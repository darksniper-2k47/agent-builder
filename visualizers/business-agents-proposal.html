Knowledge audit · 9 courses · agent-builder

You don't need a COO agent.
You need a deal desk.

I went through every _ingest pack in BUSINESS\ plus the two Hormozi packs — not the summaries, the full frameworks and SOPs files. The library is not a "business advice" library. It's a money-mechanics library: how to price a thing, how to fill a calendar, how to survive a "that's too expensive", and how to not lose the client in month two.

That maps to one new agent with six specialists — not nine agents. Here's the read.

01What's actually in the library

Nine courses, but only five distinct capabilities. Two courses are almost the same knowledge twice, and one is off your path entirely.

The spine · agency ops

GenFlow — Agency Insider

The most operational thing you own. Lead qualification (ACP/IAP, Meta-pixel check), 4-part hot outreach, DM frameworks, the two-call sale, the MVP pitch deck (never show price on call one), bottom-up pricing, 8-step onboarding, comms SLAs, weekly + monthly client reporting, the 7-step hire, the 6-section JD, SOP library governance. Explicitly anti-Hormozi on offers.

The money math

Hormozi — Playbooks + Money Models

Grand Slam Offer, Value Equation, four guarantee types, MAGIC naming. Then the sequencing engine: 5 attraction + 4 upsell + 3 downsell + 3 continuity offer types, each with real guardrails (upsell ≥4x the credit, cap Buy-X-Get-Y at 10% of base, only run Win-Your-Money-Back under 5% refunds). Plus CAC, gross profit, payback period, LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1, Client-Financed Acquisition. Plus the ad assembly line (50 hooks × 3-5 meats × 1-3 CTAs) and the Fast Cash Play.

The diagnostic

Jeremy Haynes — Million-Dollar Months

Financial modelling of a funnel step by step, then "the doubles game" — find the one stat easiest to double. Benchmarks, pixel conditioning, the hammer-them pre-call content flood, scaling cadences (10-30%/day, cost-per-X, surfing), FTC rule: never an amount plus a timeframe.

The DM close

Nik Setting — High-Ticket Coaching

Profile funnel, story-sequence structure (curiosity → obstacle → statement → belief shift → transition → close), reverse-risk DM close, perceived-value tiering + instant downsell, 80/20 productized delivery, the labor pyramid, price at 10-20% of value created.

The inbound engine

B2B YouTube + Ben Bader

YouTube sales funnel (50/30/20 buckets), the free-course VSL, the 11-question client interview, Dream 100 outreach, good-vs-bad cold DM checklists, agency pricing ladder and hiring order. Ben Bader adds the cohort method, the 7-day launch, and the sharpest copy psychology in the whole library (installing beliefs, old-way/new-way, depersonalising the negative).

The research kit

Internet Pipes

Free-tool market validation. Information hierarchy: survey data < search data < sales data. Competitor teardown via Similar Web, Wappalyzer, Meta Ad Library, 2-and-3-star reviews. Generate → Validate → Evaluate → Differentiate.

Honest flag: GenFlow and Hormozi contradict each other on offers. GenFlow says never guarantee, never use scarcity gimmicks, price bottom-up from your real cost. Hormozi says guarantee hard, stack scarcity, price to willingness-to-pay. That's not a problem — it's the most useful thing in the library. Any offer agent has to be built to hold both and pick based on whether you have proof yet. Right now, with small Cape Town trades, you're GenFlow. Once you have three case studies, you're Hormozi.

02The verdict

You already have Strategist, Content, Media Buyer, Video Editor, Dating Wingman and Agent Builder. Adding nine more top-level agents would wreck your router. The library doesn't ask for nine agents anyway.

One new agent: agents\business-agent\ — the commercial brain. Six specialist contracts underneath it, exactly like Media Buyer's specialist pattern. Two more chunks of the library feed agents you already have instead of becoming new ones.

Why one agent and not six: every specialist below reads the same three things first — who the client is, what they pay, what it costs you to deliver. Split them into separate agents and you re-derive that context six times. Keep them under one roof and the offer specialist can hand its price straight to the pitch specialist and the retention specialist.

03The six specialists

Each one is a real job you're doing badly-or-manually right now. "Does this week" is the actual thing on your plate.

01

Offer & Price Desk

Build this first

Job: turn "I want R10k for this website" into a defensible priced offer — with a scope, a guarantee you can actually honour, a downsell ladder, and a payment structure — plus the maths that says whether it's worth doing.

Knows
GenFlow bottom-up pricing (Hourly Rate × Hours + Additional Costs, then ~30% net margin; base vs base+performance) · Hormozi Value Equation, 5-step Grand Slam build, four guarantee types, MAGIC naming · Money Models CAC / gross profit / payback / LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1 · the full downsell tree (payment plan → feature downsell → trial) with its numeric guardrails · Nik's perceived-value tiering and 10-20%-of-value-created rule.
Produces
A one-page offer sheet: scope, price, what's excluded, the guarantee wording, tier 2 and tier 3 fallbacks, and payment terms. Plus a small numbers block — your real delivery cost, margin, and how many of these you need for the month.
Does this week
Prices the KANS Electrical job properly instead of "R10-15k". Builds the standing website-retainer offer you keep re-inventing per Meta lead.
02

Deal Flow (client acquisition)

New specialist

Job: fill the calendar. Qualify a lead before you spend an hour on it, then write outreach that gets a reply, then chase it on a schedule instead of vibes.

Knows
GenFlow lead analysis (site quality, socials, Google presence, is there a Meta pixel = they already spend on ads) · budget/authority/need/timeline screen · the 4-part hot outreach (problem → value → who you are → one question) · four DM frameworks with the hard rules (never sell in the first DM, never ask for a call, never send a video) · the Day 0 / 2 / 5 / 10 follow-up sequence with a new angle on day 5 · Hormozi's Core Four and Rule of 100 · Dream 100 free-work outreach · the good-vs-bad cold DM checklist.
Produces
A scored lead list (fake / bad / good / high-potential), a personalised first message per lead, the day-2/5/10 follow-ups pre-written, and a simple pipeline row per lead.
Does this week
Works the Cape Town trades you already have contact with. Turns the "hiring a marketer" and "already running Meta ads" signals into a qualified list instead of cold spray.
03

Pitch & Close

New specialist

Job: run the call. Build a pitch that's about their business, hold the price back until call two, and have a scripted move ready for every version of "that's too much".

Knows
GenFlow two-call structure and the MVP deck (10-20 slides, their logo from slide one, 2-3 problems you've spotted, objections as their own slides, never a price in the deck) · Claim / Approach / Proof / Market messaging · the four client types and how to talk to each · Nik's reverse-risk close and instant downsell · Hormozi's payment-plan downsell (7 steps), feature downsell, and the "never negotiate the price, change what they get" rule · Ben Bader's copy psychology for the framing.
Produces
A tailored deck outline per prospect, a call-one script, and a printed objection tree — what you say, in order, when they balk.
Does this week
You've been showing finished demo sites and then guessing at the money conversation. This gives the demo a spine and stops you discounting on the spot.
04

Account & Retention

New specialist

Job: keep the client. GenFlow's whole thesis is that clients leave because of bad account management, not bad work — and the first 30 days set the tone.

Knows
The 8-step onboarding (team email → welcome email → onboarding form → internal kickoff → client kickoff → admin → follow-up → execution) · the onboarding email that asks for the deposit now · the plain-English table-format agreement · comms SLAs (email within 1 business day, Slack max 2h, always be the last to respond) · meeting follow-up within 30 minutes · weekly update email with a fixed subject line + monthly report with a green/amber/red status table · Hormozi's churn checklist and the 5 horsemen.
Produces
A filled onboarding pack per signed client, a weekly update email draft, a monthly report, and a churn-risk flag when a client goes quiet.
Does this week
Every client site you've built has a CONFIRM-BEFORE-LAUNCH list and no follow-up rhythm. This is the difference between a once-off R10k and a retainer.
05

Numbers & Bottleneck

New specialist

Job: given real numbers, say which single thing to fix. Not a dashboard — a verdict.

Knows
Jeremy's funnel modelling (CPM → CTR → cost per application → show rate → close rate → AOV → ROAS) and the doubles game — find the stat easiest to double, and prefer the top of the funnel because it compounds · marketing knobs vs people stuff · Money Models payback period and Client-Financed Acquisition · Hormozi's constraint thinking (attack one thing, front-to-back, one test per week).
Produces
A one-screen model of the funnel with your real numbers in it, the named bottleneck, and one action. Refuses to answer without real numbers — that's the point.
Does this week
Feeds Media Buyer directly. When a client campaign underperforms, this decides whether it's the creative, the landing page or the follow-up — before you rebuild anything.
06

Market & Competitor Recon

New specialist

Job: before you build anything for a client or a niche, find out what's actually being searched, sold and complained about.

Knows
Internet Pipes' Generate → Validate → Evaluate → Differentiate funnel and the information hierarchy (survey < search < sales) · actively look to invalidate · competitor teardown via Similar Web, Wappalyzer, Meta Ad Library (long-running ads = winners), 2-and-3-star reviews · GenFlow's four-level competitor analysis (offer / funnel / ads / content) · Nik's Capital-Pain-Access market test.
Produces
A short recon brief: what the market searches, who's already running ads and what they say, what customers complain about, and the one gap you can lead with.
Does this week
This is the thing that found PM Technologies' buried solar line from their own photos — except systematised, so it happens every time instead of by accident.

04Two chunks that feed agents you already have

Don't build these as agents. They're knowledge, and it belongs somewhere that already exists.

→

Hooks, ads and copy → media-buyer and content-agent

Knowledge feed

Hormozi's Hooks and GOATed Ads playbooks are the strongest ad-production material in the library and they belong where ads get made — not in a new agent.

Adds
The ad assembly line (50 hooks × 3-5 meats × 1-3 CTAs, 80% of your time on hooks) · 8 verbal hook types · hook-driver by awareness level (most aware = offer, unaware = curiosity) · the 70-20-10 batch rule and the hook spreadsheet · the five ad-meat formats · CTA testing (same hook and meat, change only the CTA) · GenFlow's Ad Grid and the four copy frameworks · Ben Bader's belief installation and old-way/new-way · the FTC rule — never an amount plus a timeframe.
→

SOPs, hiring and delegation → the planned operator-agent

Knowledge feed

Real material, wrong time. You're a solo operator at 2K/month — you don't have anyone to hire yet. Bank it against the operator-agent and build it when there's a second person.

Holds
GenFlow's SOP rollout governance (only 1-2 new processes at a time, "slow down to speed up"), the process library map, the 7-step hire, the 6-section JD with a task as the filter, Asana delivery + workload boards, daily standup and end-of-day report · Nik's 80/20 productized infrastructure and labor pyramid · the content agency's hiring order and editor deadline buffer.

05Build order

One at a time, each proven on a real job before the next. Same rule as the rest of the system.

  1. Offer & Price DeskBecause every other specialist needs a price to exist. And because you're actively guessing prices on live leads right now — this one pays for itself on the next quote.
  2. Pitch & CloseYou already generate leads through Meta ads. The gap is what happens on the call. Prove it on one real prospect.
  3. Account & RetentionThe moment one of those closes. This is what converts a once-off build into monthly income — which is the actual 5-10K ZAR goal.
  4. Deal FlowOnce the close and the keep are working, turn up the top of the funnel. Not before — filling a calendar you can't convert is just noise.
  5. Numbers & BottleneckNeeds real historic numbers to be worth anything. By step 5 you'll have them.
  6. Market & Competitor ReconUseful throughout, but it's a nice-to-have until the money loop works. Build it when a niche decision is actually on the table.
What "step 1" actually looks like: agents\business-agent\ with an AGENT_ENTRY.md, ROUTER.md, rules.md, state.md, and exactly one specialist contract — specialists\offer-price-desk.md — pointed at the GenFlow, Money Models and Hormozi Playbooks _ingest paths. Registered in SYSTEM_ROUTER.md and AGENT_REGISTRY.csv. Nothing else until you've run a real quote through it.

06What I would not build

Being straight about what's in there and doesn't earn a slot.

A COO / CEO agent. You scratched it and you were right. "COO" is a title, not a job — it collapses into "answer any business question", which is what Strategist already does. The library's value is in specific, checkable procedures, and a COO agent would blur all of them into advice.
The Shkreli Pill as an agent. Genuinely good material — DCF, enterprise value, reading a 10-K, options mechanics — and completely off your path. It's for analysing public equities. There's no job in your week that needs it. Leave it in the library.
A YouTube-growth agent. The B2B YouTube material is strong, but it overlaps content-agent heavily and it's a 6-12 month play. It's a knowledge feed at best until the money loop is running.
A separate copy-agent right now. Your roster already plans one. The copy material in this library (Ben Bader, GenFlow's PAS/BAB/AIDA/4Ps, Hormozi hooks) is better used inside Media Buyer and Content Agent than as a seventh top-level agent competing with them for routing.
Anything Etsy-stealth-account shaped. GenFlow ships a grey-hat bonus PDF on fake identities, proxies and burner banking. It's flagged as off-topic in the ingest and it stays out of every agent.
