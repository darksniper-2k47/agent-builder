The client record has to live
above the agents.

You're describing one thing twice: stop re-explaining who a client is. Any agent you talk to — web, media buyer, content, video — should already know the client, what was built, what they paid, what was said last, and what they think of you.

Right now that context lives inside whichever agent happened to do the work. That's why it doesn't travel. The fix is to promote the client record to the system root, above every agent, and make reading it the first step of every boot.

02The shape

One folder per client, at the system root. It sits above every agent, so every agent can reach it.

endpoint-media-agent-system\ ├── SYSTEM_ROUTER.md ← gains a "resolve the client first" step ├── clients\ ← NEW. the shared memory │ ├── CLIENT_REGISTRY.csv the index. every agent reads this first │ ├── _TEMPLATE\ what a new client folder looks like │ ├── pm-technologies\ │ │ ├── CLIENT.md who they are. facts only │ │ ├── THREAD.md every conversation, dated, both directions │ │ ├── LEDGER.md quoted / agreed / invoiced / paid / owed │ │ ├── WORK.md what each agent delivered + real paths │ │ └── state.md where it stands. ONE next action. blockers │ ├── kans-electrical\ │ ├── deltar-electrical\ │ └── empire-digital\ reconciles both existing copies │ └── agents\ ├── business-agent\ ← NEW. reads clients\, answers commercial questions ├── media-buyer\ keeps its campaign work, points at clients\ ├── content-agent\ ├── video-editor\ └── web-agent\ not built yet — inherits this on day one
It points, it does not move. The actual website code stays in CLIENT WORK\PM Technologies\. Media Buyer's campaign work stays in outcomes\. Nothing gets relocated — that would break your builds and your Vercel deploys. WORK.md holds full absolute paths to where the real files already live. This layer is memory, not storage.

03The five files

Small on purpose. Each has one job and a clear owner, so agents don't clobber each other.

CLIENT.md
Who they are. Business name, what they actually do, contact person and number, where they came from (Meta lead ad / referral / walk-in), what they sell and roughly what a job is worth to them. Facts you'd otherwise re-type every session. Written once at intake · updated rarely
THREAD.md
The conversation. Dated entries, both directions — what they said, what you said, what was promised, what's outstanding. This is the file that answers "she said this, how do I reply." It holds the context they have of you as much as the context you have of them. Appended by any agent after any client interaction
LEDGER.md
The money. What you quoted, what was agreed, what was invoiced, what's been paid, what's owed, and the offer/price history with reasons. This is where the three PM Technologies invoices get resolved into one truth. Written by business-agent · read by everyone
WORK.md
What's been delivered. One section per agent. Web agent built X, here's the absolute path and the live URL. Media buyer ran campaign Y, here's the outcome folder. Content agent made Z. Video editor cut these. Full D:\... paths so anything can jump straight to the real files. Appended by each agent at handoff
state.md
Where it stands. Status, the single next action, and what's blocking. This is the one an agent reads to know whether to chase, build, invoice or wait. Follows the same discipline as your existing shared\pickup-handoff.md. Overwritten at every handoff

04The protocol — two lines per agent

The architecture is worthless without a rule that makes agents use it. This is the whole rule.

WhenEvery agent mustWhy
Before any work If the job names a client, read clients\CLIENT_REGISTRY.csv, resolve the name (including aliases), then read that client's CLIENT.md, state.md, and the WORK.md section relevant to this agent. Never ask Denzel to re-explain who the client is. This is the entire point. Context arrives before the question.
After any work Append what you did to WORK.md, append any client interaction to THREAD.md, and overwrite state.md with the new status and next action. Without the write-back, the layer goes stale in a week and you're worse off than now.
On money Only business-agent writes LEDGER.md. Other agents read it and never edit it. One writer means the money record can be trusted.

05The business agent on top

The memory layer makes context travel. The business agent is what you actually talk to about money.

It reads the client record, can reach into any other agent's work for that client, and is backed by the commercial knowledge in your library — Hormozi's offers and money models, GenFlow's sales and pricing, Nik's negotiation and downsell moves.

What you ask it

  • "Client said this — how do I reply?" It pulls THREAD.md, sees what was promised and what's outstanding, checks LEDGER for the money position, and drafts the reply in your voice with the right posture — chase, hold firm, or concede.
  • "What offer do I make here?" Pulls what they do, what a job is worth to them, what you've already built, and what similar clients paid. Then applies the offer frameworks.
  • "They said it's too expensive." Runs the downsell ladder — payment plan first, then change what they get. Never the same thing for less.
  • "We made this content — how do we monetise it?" Asks content-agent what exists, then works out what it's for: lead magnet, proof asset, retainer deliverable, or the front of a paid offer.
  • "The ads are doing this — what now?" Reads Media Buyer's numbers for that client and gives a verdict, not a dashboard.
  • "Brief the web agent for me." Writes the brief with client context already loaded, so you don't repeat yourself to the next agent either.

Where it stops, and Strategist starts

Strategist

Messy, new, cross-domain. Decides what to do and which agent does it. Holds you to your commitments. Already live.

Business agent

Decides what it's worth and what to say. Money, offers, pricing, replies, negotiation, client relationships. Always anchored to a specific client or a specific number.

If they ever conflict, Strategist routes and business-agent executes the commercial half. Business agent never invents a client fact — if it isn't in the record, it says so and asks.

06What I'd build first

Not all of it. One thing you can test today.

Step one: the clients\ layer plus real records for the clients that already exist. No new agent yet. Just the registry, the template, and populated folders for PM Technologies, KANS, Deltar, Empire Digital, AT Solutions, Clean View Glass, Wander — built from the CONTEXT.md files, invoices and handover docs already sitting in those folders.

How you test it

You open any agent and say "what's the situation with PM Technologies?" — and it tells you what was built, what was invoiced, what's outstanding, what's blocking launch, and what the next move is. Without you explaining anything.

If it can't do that, the layer is wrong and we fix it before building anything on top.

Then, in order

  • Wire the protocol into the existing agents' boot order — two lines each in AGENT_ENTRY.md, plus the write-back in shared\pickup-handoff.md. Media Buyer's own registry gets pointed at the new layer instead of duplicating it.
  • Build business-agent with one job to start — the reply drafter. Because "client said this, what do I say" is the thing you hit most and it proves the memory layer works end to end.
  • Add the commercial specialists — offer, pricing, negotiation — once the reply drafter is being used and the records are staying current.
The one risk worth naming: this only works if the write-back actually happens. A memory layer nobody updates is worse than no memory layer, because you'll trust a stale record. That's why step one is populating from files that already exist — the records start true — and why the write-back goes into the handoff discipline you already follow rather than being a new habit.
